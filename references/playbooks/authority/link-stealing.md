# link-stealing

**What it is.** A targeted outreach tactic where you find blog posts and listicles that already link to a weaker competitor, then persuade the writer to add you alongside them — or replace the competitor's link with yours.

**Dated field input.** An August 2026 X post reported twelve backlinks from category-listicle outreach. The accompanying screenshot showed twelve external links from eight sites, but did not expose the target, placements, link attributes, outreach denominator, follow-up count, traffic, or causality. Use the motion as a lead-generation hypothesis only. Do not repeat the source's “12 backlinks” as an expected result or benchmark. Source: https://x.com/hridoyreh/status/2085662231304229162

**When to use.**
- Your product is genuinely better than the competitor being linked to ("match and exceed" is satisfied).
- A competitor ranks #1 and has a backlink profile of review/roundup articles in your exact niche.
- You want links from pages that are already proven to write about your category.

**Method.**
1. Identify the strongest competitor for your money keyword by Googling it (e.g. search your category term, see who ranks #1).
2. In Ahrefs, open Site Explorer, paste the competitor's URL, and go to the Backlinks report. This lists every page linking to them.
3. Open the linking pages and live search results for queries such as `[category] tools`, `[category] software`, or `best [category]`. Look for a **real editorial omission**: the page serves the same audience and use case, is current enough to update, and excludes a materially distinct product that meets its stated criteria. Record the target URL, author/editor, update date, listed products, criteria, disclosure/affiliate state, current link attributes, and the exact reader benefit of inclusion. Do not treat a keyword match or missing brand name as sufficient fit.
4. For each promising page, get the writer's contact: create a Hunter.io account, enter the site/article URL, and pull the author's email address.
5. Draft a short, personal email (for the user to review and send — never auto-send): note that you saw they link to [competitor], explain you offer a better tool, link to it, and ask whether they'd be open to including you in the article.
6. Choose the ask based on verified page state. Prefer an **add-alongside** ask for a current neutral roundup where the product adds a distinct option. Ask for replacement only when the existing destination is broken, discontinued, materially obsolete, or does not meet the page's own criteria; preserve editorial choice and do not disparage a working competitor without evidence.
7. If payment, product, access, affiliate terms, or sponsorship affects inclusion, disclose it and require an appropriately qualified link such as `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow` under the publisher's policy. Never buy an undisclosed followed placement or condition value on a followed link.
8. Track replies and follow up once. Expect most messages to get no response; a small hit rate still yields high-relevance links.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Prioritize topical/audience fit, live editorial quality, stated criteria, update likelihood, contactability, and business relevance. DR and outbound-link counts are supporting fields, not the decision rule.
- "Add me alongside" fits a current neutral roundup when the product adds distinct reader value. Replacement requires a broken, discontinued, obsolete, or clearly criterion-mismatched listing.
- Only pitch claims you can prove with a live page, source packet, or test. Do not call a product “better” without explicit comparison criteria and current evidence.
- Report the actual funnel: candidates, qualified targets, drafts approved, sends, replies, acceptances, placements, link attributes, referrals, and outcomes. Do not import a third party's reply or placement rate.

**Example.** You search "AI headshot generator," see a big design tool ranking #1, and Site Explorer it in Ahrefs. In Backlinks you find a "10 best free AI headshot generators" roundup that lists the competitor but not you. You run the article URL through Hunter.io, get the author's email, and draft a note: you saw they feature [competitor], you built a stronger free tool, here's the link, would they consider adding it. The user reviews and sends. Weeks later the writer features you in the post.

**Pitfalls.**
- Pitching when your product isn't actually better — writers can tell, and it burns the relationship.
- Mass-blasting identical emails to every backlink; keep it personal and selective.
- Repeatedly following up after one unanswered reminder, unless the owner approves a different recipient-appropriate cadence.
- Auto-sending outreach. All messages are drafted for the user to review and send.
- Treating paid placement as risk-free or hiding the commercial relationship — use disclosure and an appropriately qualified link, or reject the opportunity.
