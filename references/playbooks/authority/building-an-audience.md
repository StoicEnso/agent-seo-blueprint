# building-an-audience

**What it is.** Earning backlinks by distribution: either using your own social audience so that writers and tech reporters discover and cover your launches, or borrowing other people's audiences (influencers) to get your product seen and linked.

**When to use.**
- You have, or can build, a following on a platform where tech/industry writers hang out (e.g. Twitter/X).
- You have no audience yet but can engage with or pay influencers who showcase new products.
- You're launching a product and want organic pickup that produces blog posts, Medium write-ups, or reporter coverage.

**Method.**
1. **Leverage your own audience:** when you post a product launch to your following, writers who cover your space often see it and write it up (a Medium post, an article, sometimes reporter coverage from someone who follows you). The mechanism is reach → discovery → a third party publishes about you → backlink. Post launches in a way that's easy for writers to act on (clear what it is, link, visuals).
2. **If you have no audience, borrow one.** Identify influencers who enjoy showcasing new/exciting products in your niche.
3. **Approach influencers the right way:** don't cold-DM "I built this, please feature it." Instead, engage genuinely with their content over time, mention your product naturally, and build real rapport with a few of them so coverage feels organic.
4. **Pay for reach when it makes sense:** a workable early tactic is paying a few smaller influencers (the course example: ~$200 to TikTok creators) to review the product. The goal is exposure, not a guaranteed link.
5. **Treat every share as a potential link.** The real aim is to get your product in front of many eyeballs; even one viewer among thousands who runs a blog may write about you. Any share — even a tweet — is still a signal to Google that people talk about your product.
6. For coverage that lands without a link, follow up and ask for one — see [[manual-outreach]]'s missing-link ask.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- An audience is a discovery engine, not a direct link button — set expectations accordingly.
- Even a large following barely helps with reporter source requests (it almost never works there), so use audience for organic discovery, not as a HARO shortcut.
- For influencers, relationship-and-relevance beats cold pitching every time.
- Volume of exposure matters more than the size of any single account — many small shares can each become a backlink.

**Example.** The course case: with a large Twitter following, posting a product reliably gets picked up — writers produce Medium posts and articles, and some backlinks even came from reporters who follow the account. Without an audience, the early HeadshotPro approach was paying a few TikTok influencers ~$200 each to review the product, generating exposure that turned into shares and links.

**Pitfalls.**
- Cold-DMing influencers with "feature my product" — low yield and off-putting.
- Assuming a big following converts reporters — it largely doesn't for source requests.
- Treating paid influencer reviews as guaranteed backlinks; they're exposure plays.
- Leaving link-less coverage on the table — always ask for the link afterward.
