# Evidence-Led Press Release Distribution

## Purpose

Use a press-release platform to publish **real, newsworthy company information** in a public format that journalists, partners, customers, and searchers can verify. Treat distribution as a PR/referral/citation experiment. Do not treat it as a parasite-SEO page factory or a guaranteed backlink tactic.

Google explicitly lists optimized-anchor links in press releases distributed on other sites as a link-spam example when they pass ranking credit, and it treats low-value content made mainly to manipulate ranking or linking signals as spam. Paid or advertorial links should be appropriately qualified, such as `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`.

## Source-claim assessment: openPR

The creator post claimed one free release, up to five followed links, and indexing “in minutes.” Live checks on 2026-08-16 found a real free route, but the link and indexing claims are more conditional:

- openPR's current submission page states **one free press release per 30 days**;
- that page also states **no more than five in-text backlinks**, so the five-link limit is supported as a submission rule;
- another destination-owned page observed in the same review stated a three-link limit, so openPR's own pages were inconsistent and the effective limit must be checked in the live form before use;
- it says releases do **not** go live immediately and are published on working days;
- paid credits are available for extra releases, and the service page listed paid correction/deletion;
- the service page prohibits duplicate/spam releases and content or images without rights;
- browser inspection of 15 fresh public releases found public `index,follow,noarchive` pages but no publisher-destination hyperlink in those samples; raw URLs were sometimes visible as text; and
- openPR's own SEO and Google News statements are platform marketing claims. They do not guarantee indexation, Google News inclusion, referral traffic, rankings, or link value.

The sample proves only the state of those pages at that time. It does not prove that an allowed backlink becomes clickable, followed, permanent, indexed, or valuable. Link rendering, attributes, robots directives, costs, terms, moderation, and templates are mutable and must be rechecked for each planned release.

## Source-claim assessment: MediaBoost

Two creator posts saved on 2026-08-21 report a paid MediaBoost package that produced placements on Yahoo, Slashdot, Business Insider, and other sites. The creator says most links were `nofollow`, then reports that AI mentions rose from none and search impressions improved after about two months. The same creator says he is not affiliated.

Treat this as one self-reported case, not causal proof. The posts do not provide the exact package, invoice, release URL set, pre-period, fixed query or prompt panel, Search Console export, analytics baseline, control site, confounder log, or attribution method. The placements, timing, nofollow state, AI observations, Google/Bing movement, and business impact need independent verification.

A live check of MediaBoost's own site on 2026-08-21 found vendor claims of named media placements and syndication across 500+ outlets, publication within 48 hours, permanent clickable links, a campaign report, packages starting at $389, and AI/SEO benefits. These are vendor marketing claims. They do not prove editorial endorsement, followed links, permanent index presence, AI citation, ranking change, referral traffic, leads, or sales.

A syndicated placement must not be described as independent coverage unless an editor independently selected and reported it. “As seen on” logos require truthful placement context and current trademark-use permission.

MediaBoost stays `UNVERIFIED_CANDIDATE` until destination-owned terms, package contents, outlet list, refund/correction rules, one current proof report, several live placement pages, link attributes, disclosure, indexability, permanence, and rights are checked. Do not buy or submit from this source alone.

## Hard rules

1. **Require real news.** Suitable triggers include a material product launch, original study, major verified milestone, event, partnership, award, funding update, or public-interest change. An evergreen sales page rewritten as “news” fails.
2. **Use an owned evidence hub.** Link to the canonical product, study, newsroom, or announcement page that contains full facts and remains under the project's control.
3. **No ranking or indexing promises.** Never promise a followed link, fast indexing, Google News inclusion, authority gain, traffic, citations, or sales.
4. **No optimized-anchor link stuffing.** Use the minimum links readers need, with branded, URL, or natural descriptive anchors.
5. **Paid distribution is not an SEO-link purchase.** Record the commercial relationship and verify link qualification. If a paid placement passes ranking credit and the operator cannot correct or qualify it, do not use it as a link-building tactic.
6. **No false editorial endorsement.** Syndication, guaranteed placement, and paid distribution must not be presented as independent editorial selection. Preserve required disclosure and qualify “as seen on” language.
7. **No extra mass syndication.** Do not spray the same release across additional platforms or automate cross-posting merely to create links.
8. **Truth and rights are mandatory.** Verify every material claim, date, name, quote, logo, image, trademark, and contact detail. No fake quotes, awards, partnerships, journalists, or customers.
9. **External writes need approval.** Account creation, payment, submission, publication, corrections, and deletion are approval-gated actions.
10. **Stage carefully.** Some platforms charge for correction/deletion. Complete fact, legal, rights, link, and contact checks before submission.

## Release procedure

### 1. Write the news contract

Record the event and exact date, why it matters now, affected audience, accountable company/contact, approved claims and direct evidence, approved quote and named speaker, canonical owned evidence URL, required disclosures, and facts that must not be claimed. Stop if the event cannot support a plain, factual headline.

### 2. Draft for people, not link count

Use a descriptive factual headline, direct summary, evidence and limitations, one accountable source section, working media with recorded rights, and only the links needed to verify facts or take the next relevant action. Do not manufacture length, repeat keywords, hide commercial intent, or convert the release into a generic guide.

### 3. Run pre-submission QA

Check every name, date, quote, number, product claim, URL, duplicate risk, image and trademark right, destination term, current cost, link, disclosure, contact detail, canonical owned announcement, and correction/deletion path. Save the draft with `status: "DRAFT"`. Ask for explicit approval of the exact destination, package, price, copy, links, assets, and outlet list.

### 4. Pre-register a paid one-release pilot

Before an approved MediaBoost or similar paid campaign:

1. save the exact package, price, outlet list, refund/correction terms, and checked date;
2. capture a 28-day baseline for ordinary Search Console impressions/clicks, referral sessions, named-brand searches, known citations/mentions, and a versioned fixed answer-provider prompt panel where permitted;
3. record concurrent launch, content, backlink, advertising, product, and brand activity that could affect the outcome;
4. define the owned evidence page, one real release, target audience, expected non-ranking value, measurement windows, and maximum budget;
5. stop after one campaign; do not buy a repeat package until the evidence review is complete.

This is an experiment. It is not permission to purchase or submit.

### 5. Verify after an approved publication

Capture submitted, approved, and first-live timestamps; public URLs; HTTP state; visible title/body/source/contact; canonical and robots state; each outbound URL, anchor, redirect, and `rel`; disclosure; correction needs and cost; and screenshots or page captures. Do not report “indexed” from publication alone. A `site:` result is directional evidence, not an authoritative index count.

### 6. Measure separate outcomes

Measure the fixed baseline again at 7, 30, and 60 days. Track promised versus live outlets, later removals, observable index presence, link state, qualified referrals, independent editorial pickups separate from syndication, provider-specific mentions/citations from the fixed panel, ordinary Search Console movement, named-brand demand, leads, assisted conversions, revenue, and total cost.

Publication, index presence, a followed link, a nofollow mention, ranking change, AI citation, media pickup, and conversion are different outcomes. Sequence is not causation.

## Decisions as of the checked dates

- **openPR, 2026-08-16:** `VERIFIED_CANDIDATE`, not approved for submission. The free route and five-link submission limit were observed, but clickable/followed links and fast indexing were not proven.
- **MediaBoost, 2026-08-21:** `UNVERIFIED_CANDIDATE`, not approved for purchase or submission. The creator case supports due diligence and a possible one-release pilot, not a buy, repeat campaign, or performance claim.

## Done condition

A candidate has current first-party route and terms evidence; the release is tied to real news and an owned evidence hub; claims, rights, links, disclosures, logo use, and correction risk pass QA; a paid test has a baseline, confounder log, budget, one-release cap, and 60-day review; nothing is purchased or submitted without explicit approval; and outcomes are measured separately without ranking, indexing, citation, or sales promises.
