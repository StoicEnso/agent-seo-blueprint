# haro

**What it is.** Responding to journalist/writer source requests (HARO — Help a Reporter Out — and successors like Help a B2B Writer Out) with expert quotes, to earn very high-DR editorial backlinks from publications that are otherwise almost impossible to reach.

**When to use.**
- You want a handful of elite (DR 90+) editorial links that competitors can't easily replicate.
- You or a teammate can credibly speak as an industry expert in your niche.
- You can sustain a high-volume, low-hit-rate effort over months for a big payoff.

**Method.**
1. Sign up for the relevant source network. HARO itself is less active now; the course currently uses Help a B2B Writer Out, which routes requests from writers at large companies (e.g. HubSpot, Zendesk). Sign up to receive their request emails on a daily/weekly cadence.
2. Triage incoming requests fast: each lists a topic, what the writer wants (your experience, a tip, a quote), a deadline, and sometimes the linking domain's authority. Filter to requests that match your genuine expertise and offer a high-DR link.
3. Draft a tight, credible reply (for user review, never auto-sent) with three parts: (a) a one-line credibility hook (followers, prior features, relevant role); (b) a specific, substantive answer to their exact question — real experience and concrete detail, not fluff; (c) your name and a link to back up the credibility claim.
4. Stand out from AI-generated spam by adding a human touch. The course note: record a short Loom video with your answer — AI can't do that, and a personal video makes a writer far more likely to feature you.
5. Submit before the deadline and move on; the hit rate is very low, so volume matters. Reply to many requests across topics over time.
6. When a quote lands, log the resulting link. Then nurture the relationship: a writer who liked your input may skip the request board entirely and come straight to you for future quotes, compounding into multiple links from the same publication.
7. To scale without burning your time, have a VA or content writer draft replies, then you personally review/approve/send so the quotes carry your authority — keeps the effort sustainable.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Prioritize requests with high stated DR and an exact topical match to your expertise.
- Substance + a personal element (Loom, specific data) beats generic AI quotes — that's the entire edge here.
- Treat it as a numbers game: expect many submissions before a placement, and long lead times (placements can take months to publish).
- The difficulty is a feature: links this hard to get are also hard for competitors, so each one disproportionately lifts your profile.

**Example.** The course case: a HubSpot writer requested expert quotes on AI topics with a deadline. The team's writer drafted a reply — credibility line (large Twitter following, prior features), then a concrete answer about developing in-house diffusion models, plus name and link. The user reviewed and sent it. About five months later it published on a DR ~93 HubSpot blog. The relationship led to more HubSpot placements off-board.

**Pitfalls.**
- Sending generic, AI-obvious quotes — they get ignored amid the spam flood.
- Giving up after a few tries; this only pays off at volume and over months.
- Auto-sending VA-drafted replies without your review — the quotes must be approved by the expert whose name they carry.
- Replying to off-topic requests just for a link; relevance and credibility are what get you chosen.
