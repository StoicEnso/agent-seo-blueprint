# Editorial link intent and citation-worthy assets

Use this playbook to find pages and people who already have a legitimate editorial reason to update, cite, replace, or attribute something. The operating principle is simple: **find recipient-side intent before drafting outreach**.

The source article supplied useful workflow ideas but is also a Distribb promotion. Its “37,700 AI mentions in three months” claim is undefined and not causally tied to the tactics. Its 96.55% traffic statement compresses an Ahrefs study that also identifies no search demand and intent mismatch; backlink relationships in that study are correlational. Do not repeat either claim as a benchmark or guarantee.

## Hard policy boundary

Reject any opportunity involving:

- credit-based or networked “give links to get links” schemes;
- excessive reciprocal exchange, automated CMS exchange articles, partner pages built mainly for cross-linking, or low-value content made mainly to manipulate ranking/link signals;
- money, products, services, access, or assets conditioned on an unqualified followed link;
- paid/native inclusion without disclosure and an appropriately qualified link such as `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`;
- optimized-anchor requirements, distributed widget/embed links, hidden links, or sitewide/footer placements;
- fabricated mentions, fake journalist requests, undisclosed influence, or claims that provider citations prove traffic, conversion, authority, or causality;
- auto-sent outreach. Every send remains DRAFT until the user approves the exact batch.

A useful asset may be gifted without a link requirement. Editorial owners choose whether and how to cite it.

## Opportunity classes

Use one primary class per target:

1. `competitor_listicle_gap` — a current editorial page names relevant competitors but omits a genuinely distinct option or use case.
2. `competitor_backlink_pattern` — several competitors earned links for the same replicable reason, such as research, resource, interview, or expert commentary.
3. `broken_resource` — the linked destination is unavailable or returns an inappropriate error.
4. `decayed_resource` — the URL resolves but the resource is discontinued, acquired/rebranded, abandoned, redirected to a generic homepage, or materially stale.
5. `journalist_source_request` — a verified writer/source network has an active request matching real expertise.
6. `statistics_or_research_need` — writers/searchers need current, citable facts, definitions, benchmarks, or original findings.
7. `missing_visual` — the page’s own structure/data clearly warrants a diagram, chart, comparison visual, or infographic it lacks.
8. `article_video_asset` — the publisher has a demonstrated video use case and a short source-faithful explainer could add distinct value.
9. `unlinked_brand_or_founder_mention` — the site already names the brand/founder but omits the canonical source.
10. `unlinked_owned_asset` — the site uses a proprietary statistic, chart, screenshot, quote, research result, or other owned/licensed asset without source attribution.

Do not create demand for a link where no editorial reason exists.

## Procedure

### 1. Build the evidence pool

Use Ahrefs/DataForSEO backlink rows, modest live-SERP research, current page inspection, provider-specific citation observations, verified journalist-source requests, brand/founder monitoring, reverse-image or asset searches where lawful, and owned analytics/customer evidence.

For each lead preserve the exact target URL, observed page state, retrieval date, current outbound links/citations, contact evidence, and the reason the recipient’s readers would benefit from a change.

Provider citation observations are a targeting hint only. Keep ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot, and Google Search data separate. A page being cited by one provider does not establish quality, authority, traffic, or durable value.

### 2. Classify why the existing link was earned

For competitor backlinks, classify the link reason before outreach:

- roundup/listicle inclusion;
- original research/statistics;
- free tool/resource;
- founder interview/podcast;
- expert quote/journalist contribution;
- customer case study;
- integration/tutorial;
- visual/video asset;
- broken/decayed replacement;
- sponsorship/affiliate/commercial relationship;
- unknown/non-replicable.

Copy the *legitimate editorial pattern*, not the link. Reject PBN, scraped, coupon-spam, irrelevant, manipulative, or paid-followed patterns.

### 3. Verify replacement fit for broken and decayed resources

Record the HTTP/redirect state, archive/original subject where available, exact reason the current resource no longer serves readers, and the original linking context. A replacement must satisfy the same intent and be at least as useful/current. Never offer the homepage unless it genuinely replaces the missing resource.

For decayed resources, valid evidence includes a discontinued product, acquired/rebranded entity, abandoned report, generic-homepage redirect, obsolete policy/specification, or advice invalidated by material changes. “Old date” alone is not enough.

### 4. Build a source-led statistics/research asset when justified

Route production to `workflows/content-production.md`. Require:

- a real writer/buyer query or observed citation gap;
- a source ledger with a direct source for every material number;
- primary-source preference and clear separation of primary data, third-party research, and derived calculations;
- definition, population/sample, geography, unit, collection period, publication date, checked date, and limitations;
- original synthesis, methodology, chart/table, or first-party data—do not merely copy a list of popular numbers;
- bounded quotable takeaways that remain accurate out of context;
- visible methodology and a truthful update date;
- an owner, review cadence, and invalidation trigger.

Never fabricate statistics or use a current-year title with stale underlying facts.

### 5. Use permission-first custom visual/video assets

Pre-qualify the exact target page, real contact, expected reader benefit, rights/licensing basis, and genuinely new evidence before building.

For a missing visual:

1. Count and copy the recipient’s actual labels/headings accurately.
2. Ask whether the proposed asset would be useful before costly production.
3. Build only from their public content plus clearly sourced additions; invent nothing.
4. Proofread every word, number, label, source, and brand mark against the source page.
5. Provide an accessible clean asset regardless of link outcome. If embed code is offered, use a normal source/credit link and no optimized anchor or hidden/distributed widget link.

For an article video:

1. Confirm the publisher uses video or wants the asset.
2. Preserve the article’s meaning, credit the author/source, and add genuinely new checked evidence.
3. Keep product inclusion accurate, disclosed, optional, and proportionate—not stealth advertising.
4. Verify script, captions, claims, music/media rights, and final export.
5. Give the asset without requiring a link.

### 6. Answer active journalist requests

Verify the network, publication, requester, deadline, topic, and expert fit at run time; do not assume any named platform remains active forever. Answer the question immediately, add one specific original insight or evidence-backed example, and attach a source packet/statistics asset only when it directly helps. The named expert approves the exact response. Do not turn it into a product pitch.

### 7. Reclaim uncited mentions and assets

Verify the exact mention/asset, its canonical source URL, ownership/licensing, and whether attribution is actually missing. Ask for reader-verifiable source attribution, not a keyword-rich anchor. Distinguish a fair citation request from a claim of ownership over ordinary ideas, public facts, independently created visuals, or accurately attributed quotes.

### 8. Rank opportunities

Score 0–2 on:

- editorial/readership fit;
- strength of observed recipient-side intent;
- evidence/source quality;
- replacement or asset fit;
- contactability and recency;
- distinct value over what exists;
- expected business relevance;
- maintenance/production feasibility.

Hard fail overrides the score. Do not rank by DR, dofollow status, or AI citation count alone.

### 9. Draft the ask

Use `assets/outreach-email.md` and keep the message short, specific, and value-first. State the exact page/gap, what changed or what the asset contributes, the evidence/checked date, and a low-friction editorial ask. Disclose commercial relationships. Save `status: "DRAFT"`; never send without explicit per-batch approval.

## Required opportunity record

Use `assets/editorial-link-opportunity.json`. Every record includes:

- target page/contact and retrieval date;
- one primary intent class and exact observed gap;
- link/citation state and provider scope where relevant;
- evidence URLs, checked dates, rights/source ledger;
- required asset and replacement fit;
- sponsorship/affiliate state and required link qualification;
- risk flags, score basis, draft ask, owner, next check, and status.

## Measurement

Track separately:

- researched, qualified, held, rejected, approved, sent, replied, accepted, and placed counts;
- acquired links with source URL, destination, anchor, `rel`, placement and first-seen date;
- referral traffic, leads, assisted conversions, sales/revenue where available;
- provider-specific citation observations;
- asset reuse/mentions;
- causal evidence, if any.

A link, citation, mention, ranking change, and conversion are different outcomes. Temporal sequence is not causation.

## Done condition

Every retained target has a verified recipient-side reason, evidence/rights and risk records, a matching asset or replacement plan, a personalized DRAFT ask, and an owner. Credit exchanges, unqualified paid links, auto-publishing, auto-sending, invented claims, and universal AI/authority scores are absent. Route approved sends and acquired outcomes into `workflows/monitoring.md`.
