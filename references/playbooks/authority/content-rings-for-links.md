# content-rings-for-links

**What it is.** Building and keeping a portfolio of your own free tools/sites that have accrued real domain authority, then linking from them to each new product you launch — giving every new site a backlink head start from properties you fully control.

**When to use.**
- You're a builder/programmer who can ship small free tools that earn their own backlinks over time.
- You launch new products regularly and want them indexed and trusted from day one.
- You want a self-owned, repeatable link source that doesn't depend on outreach replies.

**Method.**
1. Over time, build and maintain several genuinely useful free tools/sites in your space. Let each accumulate its own backlink profile and domain authority through normal use and the other authority playbooks.
2. Keep these properties live, indexed, and crawled — Google re-scrapes established sites periodically (every few weeks/month) looking for new links.
3. When you launch a new website, add a link to it from each of your existing ring properties. The new site instantly gains backlinks from aged, already-trusted domains.
4. Rely on the crawl cascade: because Google already ranks and regularly recrawls your old sites, it will follow the new link, crawl the new site, and start indexing it faster than it would a cold domain.
5. Treat the ring as a launch checklist item — every new product gets wired into the existing tools as step one of its backlink profile.
6. If you don't have a ring yet, start one: build a few free tools now so future launches inherit their authority. (For acquiring rather than building such properties, see [[acquiring-domain-authority]].)

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- The ring's value scales with the genuine authority of its members — these must be real, useful tools that earned links, not thin throwaway sites.
- This is your own network of properties you control, distinct from manipulative third-party link farms / PBNs — see [[linkbuilding-what-not-to-do]] for the line you must not cross (don't buy a stable of unrelated blogs to link-spam).
- Best return when properties are topically related to the new launch.
- It accelerates indexing and gives a head start; it complements, not replaces, outreach-based links.

**Example.** The course case: over years of building free tools (e.g. a landing-page resource site), each accrued a strong domain-authority profile. Now every new website launch begins by adding its link to all of these existing tools, so the new site starts with several strong backlinks. Because Google already crawls the old sites, it quickly discovers and indexes the new one.

**Pitfalls.**
- Confusing this with a private blog network: a PBN of unrelated, bought blogs linking to each other is a banning risk (see [[linkbuilding-what-not-to-do]]). A content ring is your own real, useful, individually-earned properties.
- Letting ring properties die or fall out of the index — they only help while live and crawled.
- Stuffing links between wholly unrelated sites; keep them topically sensible.
