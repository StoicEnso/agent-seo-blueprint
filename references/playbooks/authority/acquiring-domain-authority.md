# acquiring-domain-authority

**What it is.** Buying an existing website (especially an aged domain with a strong backlink profile) and channeling its authority into your main site — either by linking from it or by 301-redirecting the whole domain to yours.

**When to use.**
- You want to fast-track a backlink profile instead of building it link by link over years.
- You've found an established free tool/site in your niche with a long history and many referring domains.
- You have budget for a one-time acquisition and the patience for Google to transfer authority gradually.

**Method.**
1. Build a target list of relevant sites worth acquiring — typically free tools in or adjacent to your niche that already have a real backlink and domain-authority profile.
2. Send a polite "are you open to selling?" blast to those owners (drafted for user review, never auto-sent). Most will say no, but the point is to plant seeds: an owner who declines today may come back months later ready to sell.
3. When an owner engages, vet the asset in Ahrefs and via archive history: confirm domain age, number/quality of referring domains, and that the backlinks are genuine and on-topic (an aged, on-niche domain with many real backlinks is the goal). Avoid spammy or off-topic profiles — see [[linkbuilding-what-not-to-do]] on niche mismatch.
4. Negotiate and close a fixed sum, then take control of the domain.
5. Choose how to extract the authority:
   - **Soft start:** keep the acquired site live and add a link from it back to your main product — an immediate, controlled backlink.
   - **Full transfer:** 301-redirect the entire acquired domain to your site. A 301 tells Google "this content permanently moved here," and over time Google funnels the old domain's backlinks and authority to the destination. This is gradual, not instant.
   - **Hybrid:** move the acquired tool itself onto your own domain (so you keep the useful product/page), then 301 the old tool URL to your site so the link juice follows.
6. Monitor the transfer in Ahrefs over the following weeks/months: watch your referring-domain count climb as Google reassigns the acquired site's links.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Aged + on-topic + many real referring domains = high-value target. Young, off-topic, or spammy = skip.
- 301 the whole domain only when its niche matches yours; a topical mismatch can tank rankings (the course's fitness-blog-on-an-ex-travel-domain failure).
- Use the soft-start link first if you're unsure; you can escalate to a full 301 later.
- Expect authority transfer to take weeks-to-months; don't judge it on day one.

**Example.** The course case: a blast email to free-tool owners mostly got "not selling" replies — but four months later one owner returned, ready to sell a ~14-year-old domain with a large backlink profile for ~$8,700. After verifying age and backlinks in Ahrefs, the deal closed. The site was first kept live with a link back to the main product, then later 301-redirected entirely. The main site's referring backlinks jumped from ~100 to ~500 as Google transferred the acquired domain's authority.

**Pitfalls.**
- 301-redirecting a domain whose historical niche doesn't match yours — Google may de-rank it (the fit.nl travel-to-fitness example).
- Overpaying without verifying the backlinks are real and on-topic in Ahrefs.
- Expecting instant results; the 301 authority transfer is slow.
- Auto-sending the acquisition outreach — draft for user review.
