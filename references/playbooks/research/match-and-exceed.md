# Match and Exceed — Read the SERP, Then Beat It

**What it is.** Before committing to a keyword, look at what's already ranking in Google's top 3 and ask two questions: (1) What *type* of content does Google reward here? (2) Can I realistically create something better than what's there? Google has a specific "appetite" for each query — you must match that format, then exceed the quality.

**When to use.**
- After a keyword passes volume/KD/intent checks but before you build anything.
- Whenever you're tempted to write a blog post for a keyword — verify Google actually wants a blog post.
- When KD looks low but you're unsure you can win (KD doesn't capture content quality).

**Method.**
1. **Run the keyword on the live SERP** (google.com, set to your target country, e.g. US).
2. **Identify the dominant content type in the top 3.** Are they tools/generators? Comparison pages? Inspiration galleries? Articles? That format *is* the appetite — Google is telling you what to build. If the top 3 are all free generators, a blog post will not rank no matter how good it is.
3. **Match the format.** Decide to build the same *type* of asset the SERP rewards. Don't fight the appetite.
4. **Assess whether you can exceed it.** Inspect the top results' quality and authority: How much real content do they have? Are they thin/spammy, or polished and well-linked? Check their page-level authority in Ahrefs (URL rating / referring domains — see [[seo-metrics]] and [[competitor-research]]).
5. **Make the exceed call.** If the top results are weak (e.g. a bare Pinterest board, a thin tool, low page authority), you can win by adding depth: more/better examples, real explanatory text, a better-built free tool, stronger UX. If the top results are genuinely strong tools with heavy backlinks, winning needs authority you may not have yet — deprioritize or come back after building links.
6. **Layer in a UX/dwell consideration.** Aim to build something users stay on rather than bouncing back to Google; better engagement tends to correlate with better rankings.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Top 3 all = tools → you must ship a tool, not an article.
- Pinterest / Wikipedia / thin sites ranking top 3 = green light: easy to exceed with richer content.
- Strong, content-heavy, well-linked incumbents = hard even at low KD; needs authority first.
- Low KD alone is NOT permission to proceed — always ask "can I make better content than this?"
- The exceed lever for tools = better tool + more supporting text; for galleries = more examples + textual context + maybe a free generator.

**Example.** For `free headshot generator` the top 3 are all polished, well-linked free generators — hard to exceed without authority, so it's a long game. For `dragon tattoo idea` the top results are inspiration pages from Pinterest/Wikipedia and low-quality sites — easy to exceed by building a richer page with more examples, real text explaining dragon tattoo meanings, and an embedded free generator. Same low KD, very different verdicts.

**Pitfalls.**
- Building the wrong format (a blog post where Google wants a tool) and never ranking.
- Trusting KD as the only gatekeeper while ignoring content quality of incumbents.
- Assuming you can beat strong, heavily-linked incumbents on content alone.
- Underrating a low-quality top result — those are exactly the easy wins.
