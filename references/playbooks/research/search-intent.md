# Search Intent — Match Content to What the Searcher Wants

**What it is.** Every keyword carries an intent — the mental state of the person typing it. Misreading intent is the fastest way to fail: sending a ready-to-buy searcher to a blog post (or vice versa) wastes the click. There are four intents; for our purposes three matter and one can be ignored.

**When to use.**
- After finding a candidate keyword, before deciding what kind of page to build for it.
- When a page ranks but converts poorly — the intent and content type may be mismatched.
- When triaging a keyword list: classify each term by intent so you build the right asset.

**Method.**
1. **Classify the keyword into one of four intents** using its wording (see signal words below).
2. **Informational** — the searcher wants knowledge, not a purchase yet. Signals: `how to`, `what is`, `why`, `guide`. Build: blog posts, explainers, tutorials, reviews, videos. They're researching before they're ready to buy.
3. **Commercial** — the searcher knows they want a product and is comparing options before committing. Signals: `best`, `top`, `review`, `vs`, `compare`, `alternative`. Build: comparison pages, curated top-10 lists, before/after curation, ideally with an unbiased third-party feel.
4. **Transactional** — the searcher is ready to act now. Signals: `buy`, `download`, `book`, `signup`, `price`, plus tool/generator queries where they want to *do* the thing. Build: landing pages, booking pages, the tool itself. Convert immediately — never dump them into an article.
5. **Navigational** — the searcher wants a specific site/page (e.g. `[brand] login`). Ignore these for research; they can't be won or converted by you.
6. **Confirm against the live SERP.** Google's results for the term reveal the intent it has decided to reward — if the top 3 are all tools, that keyword is transactional/tool intent regardless of the wording (see [[match-and-exceed]]).

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Wording is the first signal; the live SERP is the tiebreaker and the authority.
- Informational/commercial keywords feed content pages and the top of the funnel.
- Transactional keywords feed landing pages / tools and convert directly.
- Drop navigational keywords from research entirely.
- Many "generator" / "maker" tool queries are effectively transactional even without "buy" — the user wants to perform an action right now.

**Example.** `best AI headshot generator` is commercial → build a comparison/curation page reviewing tools. `buy AI headshots` is transactional → send straight to a landing/checkout page. `how do AI headshots work` is informational → an explainer article. Targeting all three with the *same* page satisfies none of them.

**Pitfalls.**
- Routing transactional searchers to a blog post — they've finished researching and just bounce.
- Hard-selling on an informational query — the user isn't ready and leaves.
- Trusting keyword wording over the live SERP when the two disagree.
- Wasting effort optimizing navigational/branded terms you can't realistically capture.
