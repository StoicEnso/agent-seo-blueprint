# Measuring If Your Work Pays Off (Leading Indicators)

**What it is.** A measurement system built on **leading indicators (actions you control)** instead of **trailing indicators (results that arrive months later)**. Rankings and traffic are trailing — by the time they move, it's too late to fix the cause. So you monitor the *work* (the inputs) on a checklist cadence, guaranteeing you put in the effort that produces results down the line and never get blindsided by a drop six months from now.

**When to use.**
- Setting up an ongoing SEO maintenance routine for any project.
- Someone is "monitoring rankings" anxiously but not tracking the actions that drive them.
- Building a recurring dashboard / review rhythm (weekly / monthly / quarterly).

**Method — the action checklist + cadence.**
1. **Track six leading-indicator tasks every month** (the inputs you control):
   1. **Content shipped** — did you publish your target X pieces of content this month?
   2. **New keywords/content targeted** — are you continually going after new keywords and new content to rank for?
   3. **Existing pages optimized** — improve a few existing pages (e.g. page speed, or changing the content type to match intent — see [[keyword-intent-evolution]]).
   4. **Link opportunities identified + contacted** — find a few new link-building targets and actually do manual outreach to some of them.
   5. **Backlinks acquired** — track how much link-building work you did and how many links resulted.
   6. **Competitor backlinks tracked** — monitor your top 3–5 competitors' net-new backlinks to confirm you're out-shipping them (see [[staying-ahead-with-backlinks]]).
2. **Also monitor your most important keywords' positions** so you spot position switches early and can investigate the cause (intent shift? update? competitor links?).
3. **Build a weekly dashboard** summarizing the leading indicators — check them off so you can answer "did I do the work?"
4. **Run a monthly review** of progress against these strategies.
5. **Run a quarterly analysis** of the leading indicators against the trailing results — i.e. now look at whether ranking/traffic actually moved, and connect it back to the inputs.
6. **Pull trailing results from GSC + GA4 at review time.** Use Google Search Console for impressions, clicks, average position, and per-keyword movement; use GA4 for organic sessions, engagement, and conversions — to confirm the input work is translating into outcomes over the quarter.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Monitor inputs weekly; review outputs (rankings/traffic) monthly and especially quarterly — don't obsess over output day to day (it's trailing and noisy).
- If the six action tasks are consistently done and rankings still aren't moving after a quarter, *then* dig into the trailing data for a diagnosis (intent, updates, link gap).
- Out-shipping competitors on the action tasks month after month is, by itself, a near-guarantee of out-growing them over time.
- A surprise position drop six months out almost always traces back to skipped input work — the checklist exists to prevent exactly that.

**Example.** A founder keeps a weekly Google-Sheet dashboard with the six tasks as checkboxes: 4 content pages shipped, 3 existing pages optimized, 10 outreach emails sent, 5 new backlinks landed, competitor backlink counts logged, top-20 keyword positions noted. Monthly they review the trend; quarterly they open GSC and GA4 to see if impressions, average position, and organic sessions rose — and tie any movement back to the months they did (or skipped) the work.

**Pitfalls.**
- Monitoring only rankings/traffic (trailing indicators) and reacting too late.
- Tracking results without tracking the actions that cause them — you learn nothing actionable.
- Letting the checklist lapse and then being shocked when positions fall months later.
- Treating the weekly dashboard as the place to judge success — judge inputs weekly, outcomes quarterly.
