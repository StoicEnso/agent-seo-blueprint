# Staying Ahead With Backlinks (Competitive Tracking)

**What it is.** A monthly discipline of measuring how many *new* quality backlinks you and your top competitors each acquired, so you consistently out-acquire them. Backlink building is a marathon: win the monthly race repeatedly and within a year you're far ahead, and it would take competitors years to catch up. Fall behind and you risk being quietly outranked.

**When to use.**
- Monthly (and optionally weekly) maintenance cadence, always.
- When rankings slip with no Google update and no intent shift — check whether a competitor is out-pacing you on links.
- When scouting for new entrants who could threaten your position.

**Method — what to monitor and how to respond.**
1. **Pick your benchmark set.** Choose your top ~3 competitors plus yourself. Add a few extras if there are emerging competitors worth watching, so you're never blindsided by a newcomer.
2. **Pull new backlinks per competitor.** In Ahrefs Site Explorer, load each domain → Backlinks. Apply these filters: (a) domain authority/rating above ~25 to strip out spam and junk sites, (b) one link per domain (so you count unique referring domains, not duplicate links), (c) "new" links over the last 30 days.
3. **Count unique new referring domains** for each site in that window.
4. **Log it in a tracker.** Record each site's monthly (or weekly) new-backlink count in a Google Sheet. Keep the running history so trends are visible.
5. **Also track high-authority links separately.** A high-DR backlink (DR 80–90+) is worth far more than a low one. Keep a separate count of how many high-authority links each player earned — quality, not just quantity.
6. **Respond to the gap.** If you're below a competitor's pace, increase outreach/link-building effort this month (see [[measuring-seo-results]] for the action tasks). The goal is to consistently out-acquire — being behind on links is a leading indicator of future ranking loss.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Filter DR > ~25 and dedupe to one link per domain, or your counts will be inflated by spam and duplicates.
- One high-DR link can outweigh many low-DR links — weight quality, not just volume.
- Below competitors on monthly net-new links → expect ranking pressure soon; act before positions actually drop.
- Watch beyond the obvious top 3 — a fast-rising newcomer is the kind of threat that surprises people who only track incumbents.

**Example.** Each month a founder opens Ahrefs, runs the same filtered query (DR > 25, one per domain, last 30 days, "new") for their site and three competitors, and writes the unique-domain counts into a Google Sheet — plus a separate tally of DR 80+ wins. One month a fourth site they'd added "just in case" suddenly spikes in new links; because it was already on the tracker, they notice immediately and ramp up outreach before losing any positions.

**Pitfalls.**
- Counting raw backlinks instead of unique referring domains (one site linking 50 times isn't 50 votes).
- Skipping the spam filter, so the numbers look healthy but are full of worthless links.
- Tracking volume only and ignoring link quality/authority.
- Only watching known competitors and getting ambushed by a new entrant.
- Doing it once and forgetting — the advantage only compounds if it's a recurring monthly habit.
