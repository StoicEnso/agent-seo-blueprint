# Keyword Intent Evolution — Detect & Adapt to Shifting Intent

**What it is.** Over time, what Google (and users) consider the "right" answer for a keyword can change — the dominant content *format* in the top results shifts (e.g. from landing pages to blog posts, or vice versa). If your page no longer matches the format Google now favors for that query, it quietly de-ranks even though nothing about your page changed. This playbook is about catching that shift early and converting your content to match.

**When to use.**
- A page that ranked in the top 3 starts slipping with no Google update and no competitor surge to explain it.
- Routine monthly maintenance review of your top pages.
- Planning ahead: deciding whether to pre-build an alternate content format for high-value keywords.

**Method — what to monitor and how to respond.**
1. **Review top pages monthly.** In your SEO tool (e.g. Ahrefs Site Explorer → Top Pages for your own domain), list the pages/keywords driving the most traffic and watch for any that are sliding.
2. **Cross-check movement in Google Search Console (GSC).** For a slipping keyword, confirm the position drop and check whether impressions are holding (demand still there) while clicks/position fall (you're being outranked by a different format).
3. **Inspect the live SERP for the keyword.** Look at what now ranks in the top results. Has the dominant format changed — are landing/content pages now where blog posts used to be, or are reviews/blog posts now displacing landing pages? That format is the new signal of user intent.
4. **Match your content to the new intent.** Convert your page to the format Google now rewards. Examples from practice: turning blog posts into content-style/landing-style pages (or vice versa) when the SERP flips. Don't just tweak copy — change the *content type* to fit.
5. **Re-monitor after the change.** Track the keyword's position in GSC over the following weeks to confirm recovery, and keep it on the monthly watch list.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Unexplained drop (no update, competitors steady, backlinks fine) → suspect an intent/format shift first.
- The top 3–5 results define current intent. If they're all one format and you're the odd one out, that mismatch is your problem.
- For your most valuable keywords, it's worth keeping an alternate content format ready so you can switch fast when intent flips.
- Demand intact in GSC (impressions stable) but clicks falling = you're losing to a better-fit format, not losing the audience.

**Example.** A product's landing page ranked at the top for "best AI headshot generator." Over time Google decided searchers wanted *reviews* of options, and blog-style "best of" posts took over the top spots, pushing the landing page down. Separately, blog posts ranking for terms like "doctor headshot" and "artist headshot" began slipping as Google started favoring landing/content pages there. The fix in both cases was reformatting the content to the now-preferred type rather than fighting the trend.

**Pitfalls.**
- Assuming every drop is a penalty or backlink issue — intent shifts are extremely common and easy to miss.
- Editing wording when the real mismatch is the content *format/type*.
- Not reviewing top pages on a regular cadence, so shifts are caught months too late.
- Stubbornly keeping a format because you prefer it, when the SERP has clearly moved on (build for the user — see [[seo-philosophy]]).
