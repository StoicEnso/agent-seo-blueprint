# Vendor-Owned Commercial-Query Citation Audit

Use this when vendor-owned articles appear, or are claimed to appear, as sources for high-intent queries such as `best`, `top`, `alternative`, `comparison`, or `vs`. The opportunity is to publish a genuinely useful first-party evidence page and measure whether a named search or answer surface cites it. It is not permission to manufacture a favourable ranking or a promise that any page format causes citation.

## 1. Freeze a small commercial-query panel

Start with 3-10 questions close to a buying decision. For each row record the exact query, buyer stage, provider, surface, locale, language, account or personalization state, capture date, and business value. Version changed wording instead of overwriting history.

Do not turn a vendor's `10 articles`, `60 articles`, or backlink bundle into a quota. The panel size bounds observation; it does not prescribe page count.

## 2. Capture source ownership and result type

For each scoped run record:

- ordinary organic result, Google AI Overview, AI Mode, or named answer-provider surface;
- every visible cited URL and evidence locator;
- source owner: `own_vendor`, `competitor_vendor`, `independent_editorial`, `aggregator_review`, `community`, `documentation_or_data`, or `other`;
- page role: `alternative`, `comparison`, `best_of`, `pricing`, `use_case`, `implementation`, `review`, or `other`;
- whether the page discloses who owns the product being ranked;
- whether the brand was mentioned and whether its owned URL was actually cited.

An owned page ranking organically is not the same fact as an owned page cited in an AI answer. A screenshot from one session is a dated observation, not a stable rank. Keep observed citations separate from candidate sources.

## 3. Check whether an owned page is justified

Before briefing a page, require:

- a distinct buyer job and live demand;
- a live SERP or answer-surface format that supports the page type;
- product truth for the exact use case;
- current primary sources for product, feature, price, policy, and integration claims;
- a cannibalization and canonical-owner decision;
- material value that remains useful if the owner's brand is removed.

Reject a page when the owner's product is not a real alternative, the only change is a keyword token, or an existing page already serves the same job.

## 4. Build a fair evidence page

For an approved brief require:

- a direct answer that remains accurate when quoted alone;
- disclosed evaluation criteria applied consistently to every option;
- current sources and checked dates for material claims;
- `best for` or fit guidance instead of one universal winner;
- honest limitations and at least one material case where the owner's product loses, when applicable;
- first-party screenshots, demonstrations, data, or methodology where useful;
- clear vendor ownership and commercial-interest disclosure;
- a human-readable structure chosen for the buyer, not synthetic `AI chunks`.

Do not claim that clean HTML, question H2s, TLDR blocks, lists, freshness, or recency are provider ranking requirements without current first-party documentation or controlled evidence. Do not call a page independent when the vendor owns it.

## 5. Keep authority work authentic

A citation baseline may reveal independent sources that omit a qualified product. Route those candidates through `workflows/authority-and-links.md`. Offer checked facts, access, screenshots, criteria, and limitations. Never buy hidden placement, fabricate a review, engineer the comparison outcome, conceal sponsorship, or manufacture community discussion.

A `DR50+` backlink package is a vendor offer, not proof of relevant authority, citation, traffic, or sales.

## 6. Retest and separate outcomes

After an approved publication has had time to be discovered, rerun the same query versions on the same provider, surface, locale, and account state. A 4-8 week checkpoint is often more useful than daily noise, but use a user-approved cadence.

Report these lanes separately:

- ordinary Search impressions, clicks, CTR, and position;
- Google-specific Generative AI impressions when a real report exists;
- named-provider brand mentions and cited URLs;
- referral sessions from named sources;
- leads, assisted conversions, transactions, and revenue.

A citation appearing after publication is temporal association, not proof that the page caused it. Never multiply citations into hidden impressions, clicks, conversions, or revenue.

## Evidence boundary

The Groberman post supplied dated screenshots and examples. Live pages confirmed that SigNoz and Honeycomb publish New Relic alternative content. A published Search Engine Land analysis reported almost 8,000 unique citations across 57 queries and identified several vendor blogs among cited sources. These facts support auditing source ownership. They do not prove that vendor ownership, page structure, recency, or backlinks caused the citation.

Ahrefs' 75,000-brand study reported strong correlations for YouTube mentions and branded web mentions, while page count correlated weakly. Ahrefs also states that correlation is not causation. This argues for useful brand evidence and real mentions, not content-volume inflation.

## Rejected claims

- vendor blogs are universally preferred by AI systems;
- every best-of page trains an AI system to trust the brand;
- a fixed number of long-form posts or backlinks forms a citation engine;
- vendor pages should rank their own product first by construction;
- AI systems cannot detect vendor bias;
- freshness or a formatting block causes citation;
- one provider observation transfers to another provider;
- category ownership will occur in six to twelve months.

## Done when

The panel is versioned; source ownership is recorded for every observed citation; current evidence and disclosure are present; only justified pages become briefs; publication and outreach remain approval-gated; retests use comparable scope; and citations stay separate from Search, referral, conversion, and revenue evidence.
