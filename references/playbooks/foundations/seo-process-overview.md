# SEO Process Overview — The End-to-End Workflow (Map for Agents)

**What it is.** The four-stage loop the whole course is built around — Research, Content, Authority, Maintenance — that takes a project from nothing ("localhost") to the #1 position in Google. Use this as a routing map: figure out which stage a request belongs to, then jump to the detailed playbooks for that stage.

**When to use.**
- Orienting at the start of any SEO engagement — "where are we and what's next?"
- Routing a vague request ("help me with SEO") to the right stage and playbook.
- Sequencing work so steps happen in the right order (research before content, content before authority, then maintain forever).

**The four stages (and where to go next).**
1. **Research** — Find a niche/main keyword with real demand: high search volume, low keyword difficulty, and something you enjoy. Validate the niche, study competitors, map keyword ideas into a sitemap. Get user intent right *here*, before building anything. → Research playbooks; course chapter 02.
2. **Content** — Create content that matches the user intent for each keyword and that mirrors what's already ranking, then exceeds it. Match the format Google rewards (landing page vs. blog vs. tool vs. content page) — don't build a free tool if blog posts rank. Includes programmatic SEO, free tools, content pages, and on-page optimization. → Content playbooks; course chapter 03.
3. **Authority** — Earn backlinks and domain authority so Google trusts the content enough to rank it. Tactics: link stealing, affiliate programs, acquiring domains, HARO, manual outreach, leveraging audiences, content rings. Build a good product first — nobody links to junk. → Authority playbooks; course chapter 04.
4. **Maintenance** — Defend and grow rankings forever. Survive Google core updates, track keyword volatility, watch for shifting keyword intent, out-acquire competitors on backlinks every month, and measure leading-indicator actions (not just trailing results). → Maintenance playbooks; course chapter 05.

**Kickstart shortcut (from the conclusion).** To rank faster out of the gate, do four authority moves immediately after launching content: (1) submit to industry repositories/galleries (e.g. Product Hunt, design galleries); (2) reach out to sites linking to competitors to be added or to replace them (link stealing); (3) launch an affiliate program if you have a paid product and pitch competitor-linkers; (4) pay a few small influencers ($100–$200) to post — one might go viral and pull in backlinks. This can buy a multi-month head start.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Skipping research is the costliest error — never let content or build work start before demand and intent are confirmed.
- Each stage gates the next: great content with no authority won't rank; authority on the wrong content/intent is wasted.
- Maintenance never ends. Once ranking, the job shifts from "build" to "defend and out-compete."
- Expect a volatile early period (roughly 3–6 months for a new domain) before rankings settle — don't over-react to it (see [[navigating-google-updates]]).

**Example.** Someone says "my page dropped from #1, what do I do?" Routing: this is Maintenance, likely a keyword-intent shift or a core update. Send them to [[keyword-intent-evolution]] and [[navigating-google-updates]] rather than restarting research.

**Pitfalls.**
- Doing the stages out of order (e.g. chasing backlinks for content that targets the wrong intent).
- Treating maintenance as optional — competitors who keep acquiring backlinks will overtake a static site.
- Building for what you like instead of what users search for (see [[seo-philosophy]]).
