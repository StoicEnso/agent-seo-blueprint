# SEO Philosophy — Build Where the Market Already Is

**What it is.** Danny Postma's core worldview for bootstrapped founders: don't gamble on creating demand, find demand that already exists and build a product (and content) that captures it. SEO research is the cheapest, fastest way to confirm that demand before writing a line of product code. The end goal is a repeatable path from "localhost" to the #1 position in Google.

**When to use.**
- An agent is helping someone decide whether to start a new product, niche, or content effort.
- Someone proposes "building the market" or chasing a clever idea with no evidence of search demand.
- You need a north star to weigh trade-offs across research, content, authority, and maintenance.

**Core principles (apply these, don't just recite them).**
1. **Demand-driven development.** Validate that real people are already searching for the solution *before* building. Treat search-volume tools as a demand sensor: are people searching, how many per month, how much competition. Only build once demand is proven.
2. **Don't build the market — stand next to the busy street.** Creating market demand is expensive and slow; it's a game for VC-funded companies with unlimited marketing budget. Bootstrappers should set up shop where traffic already flows.
3. **Build for the user, not for yourself, and not to trick Google.** "You are not the person that pays for your product. Your user is." Every decision serves the user; serve the user well and Google rewards you naturally.
4. **Pick a niche you actually enjoy.** Entrepreneurship is a multi-year game. Three things matter when choosing a main keyword/niche: high search volume, low keyword difficulty, and — most important of all — that the work is genuinely fun for you. Boredom loses to a passionate competitor over years.
5. **Tell the world it exists.** "Build it and they will come" is not a marketing strategy. Distribution (content + authority) is mandatory, not optional.
6. **Depth over theory.** This is applied, battle-tested practice — what worked for Danny's startups (Headshot Pro, Profile Picture AI, Landingfolio, Tattoos AI), not textbook SEO. When experts and Danny's lived results disagree, this blueprint follows what shipped real rankings.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- No proven search demand? Don't build yet — do more research.
- A "great idea" with zero monthly search volume is a red flag, not an opportunity.
- When torn between an idea you love and one with better volume/difficulty, weight all three factors (volume, difficulty, enjoyment) together — don't optimize one to zero.
- Sleep on niche decisions. Excitement on day one often reads as boredom by day three; let ideas settle a few nights before committing.

**Example.** A founder wants to build an AI tool for an obscure hobby because they personally love it. Applying this philosophy: first check search volume for the core keywords. If almost no one searches for it, the agent advises against building — there's no busy street to stand on. If there's solid volume with manageable competition *and* the founder enjoys the space, it passes all three tests and is worth pursuing.

**Pitfalls.**
- Building first and researching later — the most expensive mistake (years spent on a product nobody searches for).
- Optimizing for Google with tricks instead of for users; it works briefly, then collapses on an update.
- Picking a high-volume niche you find boring — you'll be outlasted by someone who enjoys it.
- Treating SEO as the only distribution channel (see [[seo-and-ai-future]] and [[navigating-google-updates]] — never 100% reliant on Google).
