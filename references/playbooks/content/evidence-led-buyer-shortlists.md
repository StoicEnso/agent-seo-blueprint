# Evidence-led Buyer Shortlists

**Purpose.** A reusable brief for a vendor-owned commercial comparison page. It supplements—not replaces—the existing Vendor-Owned Commercial-Query Citation Audit and Cross-Platform Commercial-Intent Distribution playbooks. It converts a creator’s 14-step layout into a buyer-first, evidence-led page. It is not a ranking, citation, pipeline, or revenue recipe. The user’s request authorises this reusable guide and bounded research/drafting. It does not authorise a live comparison page, external outreach, purchase, or publication; those proposed actions remain **DRAFT pending exact approval**.

## Scope and claim boundary

The source is one creator post dated 2026-10-13. Its reported six-week outcome, weekly pipeline, and YouTube-ranking statement are unverified creator claims, not evidence of causality. Its word counts, update cadence, percentages, question counts, and CTA placement are useful design illustrations only—not rules or search-engine requirements.

Use the existing `workflows/category-citation-loop.md` and `workflows/geo-audit.md` to establish a real buyer job, live demand/surface evidence, a product-truth fit, a canonical-owner decision, and material reader value even if the vendor brand is removed. A vendor can publish a comparison; it must disclose ownership and commercial interest. It cannot present itself as independent editorial.

## Pre-brief worksheet

Copy `assets/commercial-shortlist-brief.json`. Record: exact query, locale/language, buyer job and stage, live SERP/answer-surface evidence and capture date, canonical URL and cannibalisation risk, products considered and inclusion rule, source owner/type for each fact, checked date, disclosure copy, CTA destination, analytics event names, and publication approval status. Use “not available” rather than filling gaps.

For every option, collect current primary evidence for price/plan, supported capability, policy, integration, and limitation. A review-site rating or count needs its direct URL, access date, plan/region context, and a note that it can change. Customer logos, testimonials, screenshots, and case-study metrics need permission or a public, accurately quoted source. Do not fabricate them.

## Page blueprint

### 1. Trust and freshness header

Show a real named author, role or relevant expertise, an optional professional photo, and a relevant profile link when truthful and consented. Show both original publish date and materially updated date. Only change “updated” after a documented substantive review. A 30–90-day review interval can be a planning prompt, not a promise; price, product, or market changes may justify earlier review.

Use one reader-visible title and matching H1, such as **Best [category] for [job] in [year]: a buyer’s shortlist**. A normal brand suffix in the HTML title is ordinary metadata when it describes the publisher. Do **not** show a different bots-only title, cloak content, hide claims, or target a distinct version to crawlers.

### 2. Direct introduction and takeaways

Open with the decision context, who the shortlist is for, the editorial/ownership disclosure, and a brief qualification for any highlighted option. Keep it as short as clarity allows; the source’s 95 words is only an illustration. Add a scannable Key takeaways H2 with five or so buyer-relevant points, each grounded in the evidence later on the page. Never state a universal winner if fit varies.

### 3. Define the category

Use an H2 such as “What [category] is—and is not.” Explain the buyer problem, boundaries, adjacent categories, and situations where the category is the wrong answer. The source’s 200–300 word range is optional. This section should prevent a bad-fit purchase, not pad keyword use.

### 4. Publish the methodology before rankings

Use “How we evaluated these options.” State inclusion/exclusion rules, date/locale, research method, sources, commercial relationship, and limits. Apply the same criteria to every product. Suitable criteria might include required workflow fit, essential feature coverage, transparent entry cost for a defined plan/region, implementation effort, support/security needs, and verifiable integration or export needs. Quantify only where comparable and sourced. A price alone is not a value score; a missing fact is not a zero.

### 5. Present options as fair profiles

Under “Best [category] options for [year],” give each option an H3 such as “1. [Name] — best for [specific job].” The ordering must follow the disclosed method, or be alphabetical/grouped rather than pretending to precision. For each option include: what it does; the buyer/use case it suits; current fact box with sources and checked date; strengths; material trade-offs; and a two-sentence Best suited for guidance. The source’s 200–300 words and suggested data fields are prompts only.

Use named founders, customers, review ratings/counts, testimonials, logos, language support, results, and case studies only if current, relevant, accurately sourced, and permitted. Do not turn a vendor’s marketing claim into an independent fact. Do not claim measurable results without a source, context, and limitation. Include at least one meaningful case where the owner’s product is a poorer fit, if that is true.

### 6. Use accessible semantic comparisons

Create a real HTML table—not an image—under Side-by-side comparison. Use table, caption, thead, th scope=col, and th scope=row; keep columns short enough for narrow screens. Each material cell needs a source or footnote. Tables must be keyboard/zoom readable and have a responsive alternative, such as grouped cards or labelled horizontal scroll, not a screenshot-only table.

A second table or decision grid can map buyer situation to an option and trade-off: “Which option fits this use case?” Avoid false certainty: use “consider” or “usually fits when,” not “choose.”

### 7. Proof, FAQ, and transparent next step

A results/case-study H2 is optional. Include only real, permissioned first-party case studies with scope, time period, baseline where available, methodology, and limitations. Link to the source case study. Do not invent testimonials, review totals, outcomes, or logos.

Add FAQs for real objections gathered from support, sales, interviews, site search, or validated query research. Six to eight short questions is a source illustration, not a quota. Answers should be direct, current, and sourced where material; FAQ markup is not a visibility guarantee.

End with a readable H2 CTA. Offer “Try free” only if genuinely free under stated conditions; say if a card, time limit, or eligibility rule applies. A free audit must describe what the buyer receives, scope, eligibility, data use, and follow-up. Use accessible descriptive link text and never pressure, obscure conditions, or imply guaranteed results.

## Conversion additions: use only when they help

1. **Early resource link.** A lead magnet can appear early if it directly answers the next buyer question, is clearly optional, and explains any form/data exchange. “10% down” is not a rule.
2. **Illustrated CTA.** A visual CTA may help if image text is duplicated in HTML, alt text describes the image rather than repeats sales copy, contrast is adequate, and the button has a clear focus state and destination.
3. **Video.** Embed a useful demonstration only where it adds first-hand evidence. Give captions/transcript, disclosure, and a text alternative. It may aid comprehension; do not claim it causes rankings.
4. **Floating/sticky CTA.** Limit to one non-obstructive control. It must not cover content, consent controls, chat, captions, or navigation; must dismiss and remain keyboard-accessible; reduce/remove it on small screens when it harms reading. Do not use a top-and-side stack by default.
5. **Natural in-copy link.** One contextually useful link near the conclusion is enough. It must accurately name the next action and disclose commercial intent.
6. **Final visual CTA.** Use only when the page remains complete without it and it meets the same accessibility, disclosure, and truthful-offer rules.

## Quality gates before any approval request

- **Intent and ownership:** fixed query/locale, live dated surface evidence, canonical decision, vendor ownership disclosed.
- **Fairness:** consistent criteria, valid alternatives, real trade-offs, product-truth review, no predetermined first place.
- **Evidence:** each material claim has a current source and checked date; owned claims are labeled as owned; independent sources are not implied where absent.
- **Honesty:** dates are real; no cloaking, hidden text, bots-only content differences, fabricated metrics, reviews, testimonials, logos, or results.
- **Accessibility:** semantic headings/tables, captions/transcript, descriptive links, responsive table strategy, keyboard/focus checks, mobile CTA review.
- **Offer and rights:** free offer is genuinely free as described; rights to marks/images are confirmed; disclosures and privacy/consent language fit the market.
- **Approval:** status remains DRAFT until exact approval covers content, outreach, payment, and publication.

## Measurement plan

Freeze a small versioned query panel and baseline before publication. Record provider, surface, locale, language, account/personalisation state, capture date, cited URLs, and source ownership. Measure ordinary-search impressions/clicks/CTR/position; provider-specific answer mentions/citations; video or native-platform engagement; referral sessions; and leads/trials/transactions/revenue as separate lanes. Do not infer one metric from another. Re-test on a comparable, user-approved cadence (a 4–8 week checkpoint can be useful, not mandatory). A post-publication change is association, not proof the template caused it.

## Release definition

The draft is ready for review when it answers the buyer’s question, remains useful without the vendor winning, names its commercial interest, supports material facts, exposes trade-offs, works accessibly on mobile and keyboard, and has a measurement contract. It is not ready because it resembles a creator’s layout or because a page has an AI-oriented label.

## Editorial operating procedure

**Research pass.** Build a claim ledger before prose. Each row should state the proposed sentence or table cell, the option it concerns, source URL, source ownership, date checked, whether it is first-party or independent, and the limitation. Resolve meaningful discrepancies rather than selecting the most flattering figure. If a vendor has region-specific pricing, show the applicable region and plan; if comparable pricing is unavailable, say so. Screenshots are supporting context, not a substitute for a stable source URL or accessible text.

**Fair-comparison pass.** Make an inclusion decision before drafting rankings. Document why each alternative is included, why obvious alternatives are excluded, and whether the publisher sells a listed product. Review the owner’s profile last with the same criteria and trade-offs as the others. If evidence cannot support an ordinal ranking, use categories such as “best for low-volume teams” or an unranked shortlist. Do not call a product “best” merely because it is the publisher’s offer.

**Copy pass.** Separate observed facts from interpretation. “The vendor lists X on its pricing page, checked [date]” is an attributable fact. “This is the best choice for every team” is an unsupported inference. Prefer bounded language: “may suit,” “consider when,” and “less suitable if.” Explain material constraints early: migration work, contract terms, feature limits, technical requirements, data residency, support coverage, and pricing changes can matter more than a headline score.

**Maintenance pass.** Keep a revision log that records what changed, why, the source rechecked, and the editor. A content-only wording cleanup does not justify refreshing a material-update date. When a material source is removed, pricing changes, a product sunsets, or a stated comparison criterion becomes invalid, mark the affected assertion for review and reduce or remove it until reverified. Do not silently retain stale winner labels.

## Example decision-table contract

A comparison table should answer a limited question, not compress the entire market into a score. A useful column set is: option; best-fit situation; evidence-backed capability; relevant entry condition; notable trade-off; checked date; source note. Define terms in a caption or nearby text. Avoid derived scores unless their formula, inputs, weighting, missing-data treatment, date, and limitations are visible. Never use decorative check marks that imply universal equivalence; a qualified “available on named plan” can be more truthful.

## CTA and measurement contract

The CTA should match the intent of the page. A buyer comparing options may need a pricing page, product tour, trial terms, or a scoped consultation—not a generic contact form. Before draft review, declare the event to record, attribution limits, consent state where relevant, and the separate destination of each CTA. A click is not a lead; a lead is not a qualified opportunity; and an opportunity is not revenue. If external distribution is later proposed, retain a distinct disclosure and approval decision for each channel.

**Reviewer hand-off.** Give the approver the claim ledger, table sources, captured query evidence, disclosure wording, proposed CTA terms, rights checks, and measurement baseline. Mark unresolved facts visibly. Approval of a layout does not approve unsourced claims, external publication, outreach, payment, or later changes.
