# Evidence-Bound AI-Referral Content Pilot

Use this small, reviewable method when a team wants to test whether a truthful buyer-question page can earn measurable provider referrals and first-party signups. It is a content brief and measurement bridge, not a promise of answer-engine traffic, citations, or customers.

## Source and limits

This method was prompted by a [17 October 2026 saved observation](https://x.com/hridoyreh/status/2111145144506352107). Its attached screenshot reports 131 **users** in 15 countries, roughly half of signups after three months, and no ad spend after comparison, use-case, and question-led pages. Those are unverified author claims. The screenshot supplies no analytics export, product identity, attribution window, paid-customer count, or controlled test. Do not restate it as 131 customers or causal proof.

A provider citation is not a referral. A referral is not a signup. A signup is not a paid customer. Referrer host alone cannot identify a prompt, cited page, model, or cause.

## When to use it

Start only after a fixed buyer question has an observed provider-answer or ordinary-search gap, a verified product-truth contract, and a plausible existing-page reuse candidate. Use one to three pages in a pilot. Do not turn variants into a page factory.

Route provider observations through workflows/ai-answer-visibility-loop.md, ordinary-search opportunities through workflows/monitoring.md, and a build or publish decision to the owner approval path. This playbook does not authorize external writes.

## Method

1. **Freeze the question.** Give each question an ID, version, exact wording, audience, locale, and evidence source. Preserve unknown demand. The source examples “free typeform alternative for scoring” and “quiz tool for lead qualification” are examples from the screenshot, not validated queries for another product.
2. **Classify page role and truth boundary.** Choose one role: alternative, comparison, use_case, or question_answer. State the reader problem, material trade-offs, product facts, competitor/source date, unknowns, and prohibited claims. An alternative or comparison page needs current, fair evidence for every material claim; otherwise choose a use-case or question-answer page.
3. **Reuse before creating.** Inspect canonical, intent, current traffic/query evidence, internal links, and overlap. Prefer a specific improvement to an existing page when it can answer the question without confusing its intent. Record why a new page is distinct when reuse is rejected.
4. **Write one bounded brief.** Copy assets/ai-referral-content-pilot.csv into the project workspace. One row represents one question/page decision. Include the approved CTA, a useful answer before the offer, reader-relevant evidence, and the planned canonical URL. A comparison must help a reader choose, not manufacture a competitor slug.
5. **Pre-register evidence review.** Set a reporting window and time zone before any change. Preserve provider, surface, question version, landing page, referrer scope, cohort definition, deduplication key/rule, and consent/privacy limits. Use first-party reporting only; do not export raw event identifiers into the brief.
6. **Review separate lanes.** Compare each lane with its own units and denominators: provider citations/mentions, referral sessions, eligible first-party signups, and paid customers/revenue. Report zero, missing, and unknown values as such. Do not sum them or infer one lane from another.
7. **Decide after the window.** Mark adopt, revise, hold, or inconclusive against the pre-registered decision rule. Temporal order is not causal proof. Keep content cost and maintenance separate from ad spend; “zero ad spend” does not mean zero cost.

## Compact example

The template row below is illustrative, not a live observation or a published-page decision. Keep its current canonical URL on the existing page until reuse has been reviewed. Replace fixture fields only with checked project evidence.

In a hypothetical project, sales research identifies a buyer question that asks whether a product can score inbound leads without a long form. The team observes a fixed provider answer but no owned page that directly explains the workflow. It checks an existing lead-qualification guide first. If that guide can add a distinct, sourced scoring section without intent conflict, the pilot records an update. If not, it briefs one question-answer page. It records provider observations and first-party referral sessions separately for a four-week Europe/London window, then reviews deduplicated eligible signups without claiming the provider caused them.

## Required artifact fields

The template requires question and evidence provenance, page/reuse disposition, claim and competitor evidence, content approval, reporting window/time zone, attribution scope, units, cohort, deduplication, separate observed measures, limitations, and decision state. Empty evidence fields remain empty or unknown; they are not filled with estimates.

## Guardrails

- No invented competitors, irrelevant alternative pages, fake reviews, unsupported integrations, or copied source text.
- No claim that an answer engine sent traffic or signups without first-party evidence scoped to the reporting window.
- No claim that a referrer proves a prompt, citation, model, ranking, or causal lift.
- No publishing, analytics configuration, outreach, account change, or schedule change from this template.
- No personal data, raw event IDs, or private analytics captures in public content or repository artifacts.

## Done when

A small pilot has a frozen question, defensible page role, reuse decision, approved product-truth boundary, and a dated review plan with separated evidence lanes. It is ready for human review, not automatically for build or publication.
