# Content Pages (and the Five Sub-Patterns)

**What it is.** A content page is a hybrid of a landing page and a blog post: it carries blog-level depth and answers questions, but is laid out with landing-page structure and components (distinct sections, screenshots, pricing/testimonial blocks). It targets informational and commercial-investigation intent that wants rich, structured content rather than a plain article. Five recurring sub-patterns each map to a specific keyword modifier.

**When to use.**
- The SERP for your keyword shows structured, content-rich pages (often from vendors themselves) rather than plain blog posts.
- You want to own a query where competitors or affiliates would otherwise control the narrative (reviews, comparisons).
- A keyword carries a modifier: alternative/vs/comparison, review/case study, a niche qualifier, examples/how-to, or "X for [platform]".

**Method.**
1. Identify the keyword modifier and pick the matching sub-pattern (below). For SaaS alternative, comparison, integration, constraint, pricing, use-case, problem, feature, or audience terms, apply the evidence gates in `content-types-overview.md` before assigning a URL.
2. Read the top 3 to confirm Google rewards content pages here (these SERPs frequently swing between blog posts and content pages — verify live).
3. Build a structured page: clear sections, supporting screenshots/visuals, distinct blocks for pricing/testimonials/data, and copy that answers the searcher's questions.
4. Load it with the niche/long-tail keywords you don't want on your homepage, so it ranks without cannibalizing the homepage.
5. Internally link it to related pages and your product; add your offer/CTA so the page converts.

**The five sub-patterns.**

1. **Comparison and alternative pages** (keywords: *alternative, vs, comparison, compare*). Help a buyer judge replacement fit or differences between products with explicit criteria, current cited product/pricing evidence, screenshots or demonstrations, limitations, and material rows where the owner's product loses. Own the page to supply first-party evidence, not to manufacture a predetermined winner. `Free`, `open-source`, `cheaper`, and audience-specific alternative claims must be literally true for the scoped use case.

2. **Success stories / reviews** (keywords: *review, case studies, customer stories*). Build your own "[Product] review" page listing your positive reviews, Trustpilot, and user examples; and/or a customer success-stories/case-studies hub. Why own it: capture the "is [product] trustworthy?" searcher and convince them with your own curated proof, rather than ceding the position to a competitor-funded negative review.

3. **Niche content hubs** (keywords: industry/niche qualifiers). Groups of pages hyper-focused on a niche within your business, e.g. headshots → actor headshots, doctor headshots, real-estate headshots. Each page is loaded with niche-specific keywords you deliberately keep OFF the homepage to avoid clouding/cannibalizing its keywords. Note: these were once blog posts, but Google switched the intent toward content pages — so they were rebuilt as structured content pages.

4. **Example / how-to pages** (keywords: *examples, how to, use cases*). A directory of use cases, tutorials, and examples of using your product, e.g. SEMrush's examples marketplace ranking #1 for "SEMrush examples". Keeps the searcher on your property (instead of a third-party blog) and funnels them toward a trial.

5. **Integration pages** (keywords: *X for [platform]*, e.g. "pop-ups for Wix", "email software for Shopify"). Explain a working supported integration or reproducible workflow with setup steps, supported actions, limitations, screenshots or demonstrations, and current documentation. Do not create a page merely because a platform has search volume. Repeated pages need distinct integration truth and user value, not a swapped partner token.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Pick the sub-pattern by the keyword modifier; build the matching page rather than a generic blog post.
- Treat the SaaS page-family list as candidate generation, not a fixed build order. Live demand, SERP format, product truth, distinct value, and evidence decide what ships.
- Use niche/comparison/review pages to control narratives competitors or affiliates would otherwise own.
- Push niche keywords onto dedicated pages, never onto the homepage — protects homepage focus and avoids cannibalization.
- Integration and example patterns scale: template once, repeat across platforms/use-cases (a bridge into programmatic SEO).

**Example.** SEMrush ranks #1 for "SEMrush examples" with its own examples marketplace; Ahrefs holds a top spot for "Ahrefs vs SEMrush" with a comparison content page so affiliates don't hand the recommendation to SEMrush; HeadshotPro runs niche hubs for actor/doctor/real-estate headshots with keywords kept off the homepage; PopupSmart runs an integration directory ("pop-ups for [platform]") repeated dozens of times. All are structured content pages, not blog posts.

**Pitfalls.**
- Putting niche keywords on the homepage and cannibalizing it.
- Assuming the format is fixed — these SERPs flip between blog posts and content pages; re-check.
- Building a thin page; content pages must be genuinely content-rich.
- Hiding unfavorable comparison facts, using stale competitor pricing, claiming an integration that is only aspirational, or publishing near-identical audience/company-size variants.
