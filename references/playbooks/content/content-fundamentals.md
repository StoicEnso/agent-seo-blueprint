# Content Fundamentals (Match-and-Exceed for Content)

**What it is.** The core rule that governs every piece of SEO content: you are serving two audiences at once — the human searcher and Google's ranking system — and the fastest way to satisfy both is to look at what already ranks for a keyword and produce something that matches that format and beats it on value.

**When to use.**
- Before deciding what *type* of content to build for any target keyword.
- Whenever you are tempted to pick a content format based on assumption instead of evidence.
- As a sanity check before spending money/credits generating content at scale.

**Method.**
1. Take the target keyword and search it directly in Google (the live SERP is the source of truth, not your intuition).
2. Read the top 3 organic results and classify the *format* Google is rewarding: landing page / free tool, content page, article or blog post, forum thread (Reddit etc.), comparison page, directory.
3. Infer the user intent from that format: tools + landing pages = transactional; articles + forum posts = informational; comparison/review formats = commercial investigation.
4. Decide your content type to MATCH the dominant format. Do not fight the SERP — if Google shows articles, a landing page will not rank, and vice versa.
5. EXCEED the incumbents: produce the same format but with more value — better data, more complete coverage, faster tool, clearer structure, better visuals.
6. Pair the content with the right authority (backlinks) so it can actually climb. Great content without links rarely reaches the top.
7. Re-check the SERP periodically; Google shifts what it rewards for a keyword over time (e.g. swinging from blog posts to landing pages for the same query), so a format that ranked last year may be wrong now.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- "AI headshot generator" vs "best AI headshot generator" look almost identical but resolve to opposite intents — the first surfaces tools/landing pages, the second surfaces articles and Reddit. Always test the exact phrasing.
- If the SERP for your keyword is all articles, building a landing page is wasted effort (and money). Choose the format Google already endorses.
- Two parties, one test: if the content genuinely helps the searcher and is better than what ranks, Google has a reason to promote it.

**Example.** For "AI headshot generator" the top 3 are a free-tool landing page, a paid-tool landing page (the operator's own), and a competitor's paid-tool landing page — purely transactional, so you build a landing page. For "best AI headshot generator" the top 3 are a journalist's article, an r/headshots Reddit thread, and a major-publication article — informational, so a landing page here is a guaranteed waste; you'd need an article or a placement instead.

**Pitfalls.**
- Picking a format from gut feel instead of reading the SERP.
- Assuming intent is fixed; it changes with Google updates (covered in maintenance).
- Generating expensive content at scale before confirming the format that ranks.
