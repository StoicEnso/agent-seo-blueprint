# Articles & Blog Posts

**What it is.** Long-form written content for purely informational intent. It can rank well — but only when it is genuinely good, human-written content; AI-generated or shallow articles backfire.

**When to use.**
- The SERP for the keyword is dominated by articles/blog posts (clear informational intent).
- You (or a hired writer) will actually invest in quality writing with original research and examples.
- Skip articles entirely if you can't commit to quality — they are not the default answer to every keyword, and a developer-led operation may rationally use zero blog content.

**Method.**
1. Confirm informational intent via the SERP (articles ranking on top).
2. Decide honestly whether you'll produce quality writing. If not, choose a different format (landing page, free tool, content page) that plays to your strengths.
3. If yes, hire a professional copywriter/content writer who enjoys writing, can do original research, and writes entertaining content with real examples.
4. If you use AI at all, use it only to draft fast, then have a human heavily rewrite to add depth, voice, and value — never publish raw AI output.
5. Apply on-page basics and avoid keyword stuffing.

**Decision criteria / heuristics.**
- Users can tell AI-written content within a few sentences even when Google initially can't — and shallow content loses ranking once both catch on.
- Articles only win on user intent + genuine value. Right keywords with shallow content may bump briefly, then drop.
- Honest self-assessment beats forcing a format: it's fine to build a content strategy with no articles if writing isn't your strength.

**Example.** A cautionary case: an AI-copywriting agency produced 13 articles that were published, after which the site's rankings collapsed and stayed down for about a year — Google and users recognized the content as shallow/AI-generated. The takeaway: AI article spam actively harms a site; either invest in real writing or don't publish articles.

**Pitfalls.**
- Publishing AI-generated articles at scale (ranking-killer; see content-what-not-to-do).
- Treating articles as the universal default instead of matching format to intent.
- Keyword stuffing to compensate for thin writing.
